      The Wrong Way

      About

      The idea

      Two pins, one city, and a lot of tempting wrong turns. Draw the shortest route from A to B along the real streets. When you submit, the game runs Dijkstra's algorithm on the very same road network, rules included, and shows you the best way and where yours split from it.

      How to play

      • Click a ringed intersection next to your line's end, or press and drag along the streets.
      • Dragging back over the previous junction undoes a step. Roads can't be skipped and routes can't loop.
      • On a keyboard, focus the map, then use the arrow keys to take the road that points that way. Backspace undoes and Enter submits once you are at B.
      • Ctrl/Cmd + Z undoes. Clear route never touches the map or the pins. An unfinished route is saved, so a refresh or the Back button won't lose it.

      Road rules

      Some maps have a river you can only cross at bridges, roadworks and accidents that close a road, and one-way streets marked with arrows. They are real rules: you can't draw through a closed road or against an arrow, and the optimal route obeys them too. Every destination stays reachable.

      Scoring

      efficiency = optimal ÷ yours × 100 and points = 1000 × (efficiency ÷ 100)². A route as short as the optimal one is Perfect and scores 1,000. Any longer route stays just under that, 99.9% and 999 points at most, so what you see always matches what is recorded. Distances are in fictional metres, derived from the street geometry.

      Medals: Finisher for reaching B, Bronze 80%+, Silver 92%+, Gold 98%+ and Perfect for the optimal route.

      Modes

      The Daily Challenge is one deterministic classic city per local day, identical on refresh, saved in this browser only. Practice generates endless new maps, with the rules of your choice, and keeps its own statistics. Journey is fifteen fixed levels in five chapters that introduce rivers, roadworks and one-way streets one at a time. Nothing syncs between devices, and no accounts or network are involved.
